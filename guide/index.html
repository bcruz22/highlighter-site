Make your first reel

Highlighter turns your game clips and photos into a highlight reel cut to the beat of a song. You pick the clips and the song; it finds the big moments, lands them on the beat, and adds the effects. Then you post it to Instagram with the same song.

Before you start

  • Have your clips in Photos. Short videos of the plays (a few seconds each) and photos work best. Videos shot at 60fps or more get the smoothest slow motion.
  • Allow access to your photos when asked. If you choose "Limit Access", Highlighter can only use the photos you allowed. It tells you if a pick was left out and lets you allow more.
  • Apple Music is optional. With it, you hear the full song while editing. Without it, you hear Apple's 30-second preview, and everything else still works.

Make a reel

  1. Tap New Reel and pick your clips and photos. If you pick more than 40, Highlighter keeps the 40 best moments.
  2. While they import, choose a song. Search by artist or title, or tap Show Apple Music Top Charts.
  3. Drag the song under the red box to choose the part you want, or tap Use Best Part. Tap Play to hear it. The big time at the top is where to start the song in Instagram.
  4. Tap Use Song, or Skip to add music later.

Your reel opens already cut: a quick tease of the best moment first, the best play on the song's drop, and every cut on a beat.

Bringing in a lot of clips, or clips stored in iCloud, can take a while. A bar shows the progress, and Cancel stops it.

Find your way around

  • The preview plays the finished look with the song. Tap it to play or pause.
  • The blue dot next to the time pulses on every beat.
  • The metronome button adds a tick on every beat, so you can hear whether a cut lands on it.
  • The strip under the preview shows your clips in order, with the song's beats as blue ticks. Tap a clip to jump to it; drag a clip onto another to move it.
  • The name at the top renames the reel. Tap it.

Change one clip

Tap a clip in the strip. The tools along the bottom switch to that clip:

Trim
Drag the white handles on the clip, or open Trim and use Start Here and End Here. This Is the Moment marks the strike, save or tackle so it lands on the beat.
Impact
What happens on the moment: Punch (zoom and flash), Shake, Slow-Mo, Freeze, Spotlight (your player lit, the rest dimmed), Replay, Speed-Up or Reveal (starts tight and pulls out). Below that, how the clip cuts in: Cut, Whip or Dissolve.
Frame
Drag to choose what's in frame. Follow a Player: tap your player once and the frame follows them through the clip. You can also show the whole picture over a blurred background.
Sound
Videos only, when game sound is on. Tap to switch the clip to Muted and leave its sound out (a coach shouting, wind noise); the rest of the reel keeps theirs. Tap again to bring it back.
Delete
Removes the clip. The reel stays on the beat.
Done
Back to the reel's tools.

Every change stays on the beat. You can't knock a cut off the beat by trimming or moving clips.

Change the whole reel

Auto-Edit
Re-cuts the whole reel in a new style every time you tap it: different photo lengths, cuts, hits and photo moves. Clips stay in match order and the best play stays on the drop. Tap until you like the take; changing the length or song later keeps that style. It replaces changes you made by hand.
Text
Player name and number (a strap along the bottom), an end card with name, number, team and season, and an optional title card at the start.
Look
Color styles (Matchday, Night Lights, Clean, Mono) and their strength. Also: automatic lighting fixes, photo motion, cinematic bars on the opening shot, and game sound (the real strike and crowd around each big moment).
Length
Auto, 15s, 30s, 60s or 90s. Shorter keeps only the best moments; longer shows more of each play.
Formats
Vertical 9:16 for Reels and Stories, Portrait 4:5 for the feed, Square or Landscape. Export makes one video for each you pick.
Add
Adds more clips or photos. They go in where they happened, on the beat.

Pick the song's moment

Tap the song at the top of the editor (or Add music) to change the song or the part you use.

  • The bar across the top is the whole song. Tap or drag it to jump anywhere.
  • The waveform below it is where you fine-tune. Drag it, and the reel re-cuts to the new beats when you go back.
  • If you see "Line It Up", Highlighter couldn't tell where Apple's preview sits in the full song, so its Instagram start time may be off. Tap Line It Up and follow the steps. With Apple Music, slide the preview until Compare sounds the same, then Check the beat. Without it, type the time where the preview's opening plays in Instagram.

Export and post

  1. Tap Export, then Export Reel. Keep Highlighter open until it's done (the screen stays on by itself); a bar shows how far along it is, and Cancel stops it without saving anything. The video saves to Photos.
  2. Tap Share to Instagram. The start time is copied for you.
  3. In Instagram, add the same song and set it to start at that time. The cuts land on the beat.

The reel itself has no music in it, only game sound if it's on. That's on purpose: Instagram supplies the song, so your post isn't muted for copyright.

Export → Save Photos… saves the reel's photos framed like the video. Tap the ones you want or Select All; they work well as a carousel.

Your reels and settings

  • Every reel is saved on the home screen. Tap one to keep editing.
  • Press and hold a reel to Rename or Delete it. Deleting a reel never touches your photos.
  • If a reel can't be opened (a damaged file, for example), the home screen says so. The reel isn't deleted; it stays on your phone so an update can open it.
  • The ⋯ menu → Settings sets defaults for new reels: formats, look, length, your player details, and the small Highlighter mark in the corner.
  • Settings → Storage shows the space Highlighter uses and frees it: leftover temporary files, clips that aren't in any reel, and previews (each reel makes its previews again from Photos the next time you open it). Your photos and videos in Photos are never touched.

Known limits, feedback and ideas

  • Apple only shares a 30-second preview of each song. Beats outside it are worked out from the tempo, which works best for songs with a steady beat.
  • Some songs can't be placed in the full song automatically. You'll see Line It Up when that happens.
  • Reels and settings stay on your phone. They don't sync between devices.
  • If you leave the app while a reel is exporting, the export can stop. Highlighter tells you when that happens; just export again.

Send feedback from the app: take a screenshot while using Highlighter, tap Share Beta Feedback, and say what you expected to happen. Crashes are reported automatically.

Have an idea? Suggest a feature. It takes about a minute, and you can add screenshots, videos or files.
